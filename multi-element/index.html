    A multi-element airfoil¶

    For a slat, main element and flap the solver can generate the grid ("grid": "multi_element", below); for any other geometry with coves and gaps, make a structured multi-block grid with your mesher and point the case at the file. The worked example is examples/30p30n_sa: the 30P30N three-element airfoil on JAXA's 117-block grid (download instructions in data/grids/README.md).

    1. What the grid file must be¶

    • Format: Plot3D (any variant: formatted or binary, single or double precision, 2D, or 3D with one plane used) or CGNS saved in HDF5. Pointwise, ICEM CFD and most structured meshers write one of these.
    • Blocks meet node to node. Interfaces are found by matching coincident block faces, so no connectivity file is needed, but the nodes on both sides of an interface must coincide. Non-matching (patched) interfaces are not supported.
    • Every wall closes round its body, and the outer boundary closes round everything.

    In a CGNS file, boundary names come from the file (family names, else BC types). Plot3D has no names, so faces that are not interfaces are named by the loop they lie on: the outer loop becomes farfield, and each closed loop round a body becomes wall. For other cases, such as a symmetry plane, an inflow or an outflow, name faces with box rules in domain.patches (the flat-plate example does this).

    2. case.json¶

    {
    "general": {"case_name": "30p30n_sa"},
    "domain": {
        "grid": "file",
        "file": "data/grids/2Ddomain_L1_coarse_r1.p3d",
        "coordinate_scale": 0.05555555555555555
    },
    "freestream": {"mach": 0.17, "alpha_deg": 5.5, "reynolds": 1.71e6, "temperature": 295.56},
    "material_properties": {"dynamic_viscosity": "sutherland", "sutherland_temperature": 110.4},
    "turbulence": {"model": "sa_noft2"}
}

    Units. The solver works in the length your Reynolds number is based on, and the force coefficients use the same length. JAXA's grid is in inches, with an 18 inch stowed chord, so coordinate_scale is 1/18. If your grid is already in chords, leave it out. If you would rather keep the grid as it is and quote coefficients per some other length, set reference_length; the Reynolds number is then per that length too.

    The pitching moment is taken about (0.25, 0) in the scaled coordinates.

    Look at three things. The number of bodies should be the number of elements: if it is lower, a wall did not close, usually because two blocks meet with nodes that do not coincide. The body extents should be in chords. And grid.png should show every element outlined in its own colour:

    Bodies are numbered by their leading edge, from the front: here slat 0, main 1, flap 2.

    4. Run¶

    jaxaero examples/30p30n_sa

    Use the implicit solver with van_albada; high-lift flows at low Mach number converge slowly, so give it several thousand iterations (8000 here). The coarsest 30P30N level (63,957 cells) runs its 8000 iterations in 7 minutes on an A100 (49 ms each); its forces keep moving by a little more than the forces_tol bar, so it does not stop earlier. On a small GPU, run a grid of this size on a cluster instead. Its block sizes are odd, so grid_sequencing refuses it.

    The forces are printed for the whole configuration and for each body. On this coarsest level, at 5.5 degrees:

    CL CD
    slat (body 0) 0.151 -0.065
    main (body 1) 2.183 -0.135
    flap (body 2) 0.453 0.259
    total 2.787 0.059
    JAXA's RANS, for comparison 2.877 0.040

    On the third grid level (261 thousand cells) the lift comes within 0.03 % of JAXA's. Per-element drag is the difference of large pressure forces and depends strongly on the grid.

    *_surface.dat lists Cp and Cf body by body, each starting from its trailing edge, with a body column to tell them apart.

    When the grid is refused¶

    • a face coincides with more than one other face: two blocks overlap, or a block is duplicated in the file.
    • block k folds over: that block has cells of both orientations; fix it in the mesher.
    • no boundary condition for patch(es) [...]: the file names a boundary the case does not mention, and the name has no default. Add it to boundary_conditions; the message lists the grid's patch names.
    • a 1-to-1 interface the file declares is not matched: a CGNS file says two faces connect, but their nodes do not coincide.

    Generated grids¶

    "grid": "multi_element" builds the multi-block grid of a slat, main element and flap from their coordinates alone, in about a minute on a CPU. The examples are examples/main_flap_sa (the 30P30N without its slat, one file per element) and validation/cases/gen_30p30n_a5p5 (the full 30P30N from one curve file).

    When it applies¶

    • The arrangement: a main element with a flap behind it, a slat ahead of it, or both (main+flap, slat+main, slat+main+flap, listed front to back), each trailing edge over the nose of the element behind it, as deployed in a single-slotted high-lift section. Not covered: two slats or two flaps (double- and triple-slotted systems), Krueger flaps, vanes, tabs, or elements side by side; for those, mesh with your own tool and use "grid": "file".
    • Each element a closed contour that does not cross itself or another element. Sharp or blunt trailing edges (a blunt base of any thickness), smooth or stepped coves, a cusp on the slat: all taken.
    • Gaps wide enough for the near-wall layers: each layer is at least 1.5 wall_spacing x layer_cells thick and at most 0.3 of the distance to the nearest other element, so a gap needs at least 5 wall_spacing x layer_cells (0.24 % of the chord at the defaults, 1.5e-5 and 32).
    • Units: coordinates in the length the Reynolds number is per, the section between 0.1 and 10 of it along x; otherwise set coordinate_scale.
    • Settings measured to build (round the 30P30N's, below): flap deflection 20 to 40 deg, gap 0.5 to 3 % of the chord, overlap -1 to +0.14 % (+2 % with a 3 % gap); slat deflection 20 to 30 deg, gap 1.5 to 4 %.

    Outside these the grid is refused with the reason (below), never built folded.

    The coordinate files¶

    Either one file per element (elements[k].file), in any format airfoil takes:

    30P30N flap element          <- an optional name line
1.1278162  -0.1459988        <- x y, one point per line, round the contour
1.1253447  -0.1449002
...

    Selig (round the contour, starting at a trailing-edge point), Lednicer (the two point counts, then the upper and the lower surface from the leading edge) or a plain two-column table, in either direction. A blunt trailing edge is left open: the file runs from one end of the base round to the other, and the base is the straight line between them.

    Or one curve file for all elements (domain.file, elements then roles only):

    33                           <- a curve: its point count,
15.730081  0.551473  0.0     <- then its points, x y (a third column is ignored)
...
9                            <- the next curve
...

    Curves join end to end, in any order and direction, into closed contours, one per element; the elements are taken front to back by their leading edges. A blunt base is its own curve (or part of one); it is found as the straight piece between the two sharpest corners at the trailing edge.

    In both, the elements are as deployed, in one frame, x downstream and y up. Corners (points where the contour turns more than 30 deg: a cove step, a cusp, a base's corners) are kept as nodes, the surface is splined between them, so give the corners as points of the file. A slat or flap may be re-placed instead of re-drawn (deflection_deg, gap, overlap below).

    Case keys¶

    "domain": {
    "grid": "multi_element",
    "elements": [{"role": "main", "file": "main.dat"},
                 {"role": "flap", "file": "flap.dat", "deflection_deg": 5.0, "gap": 0.015}],
    "wall_spacing": 1.5e-05,
    "cells_per_chord": 150
}
    • Re-placing a slat or flap: deflection_deg rotates it further deployed from its given position (flap trailing edge down about its leading edge, slat leading edge down about its trailing edge), then gap (from the upstream trailing edge to the downstream element) and overlap (along the main element's chord, positive when they overlap) set its position; leave either out to keep it. The main element stays where it is.
    • Resolution: cells_per_chord on the walls (clustered at edges and corners), wall_spacing for the first cell (1.5e-5 chords is y+ ~ 1 at Re 1.71e6), layer_cells and layer_thickness for each element's near-wall layer (thinner in gaps and coves), gap_cells across a gap at least. Every block's cell counts are multiples of even_cells (4: two grid-sequencing levels). Every key: the case reference.

    Start high-lift cases from freestream (grid_sequencing 0): coarsened 30P30N grids settle on a separated flow (CL 1.5-1.6, CD 0.3-0.4) that the fine grid then takes thousands of iterations to wash out.

    What is built¶

    Each element has a near-wall layer, j from its wall; a front block wraps the first element's nose, a block runs over the first element and its wake, a slot block over each downstream element under the upstream wake (its left side the gap), a cove block in each cove, and blocks under the elements, split at each cove lip; a ring carries the grid to far_field. python scripts/plot_blocks.py CASE --window x0 x1 y0 y1 draws the blocks.

    When the grid is refused¶

    message meaning
    no multi-element template for elements [...] roles not in one of the three orders, front to back
    the section spans ... along x coordinates in another unit: set coordinate_scale
    ... near-wall layer would be ... thick ...: too thin a gap narrower than the layers need: lower wall_spacing x layer_cells, or open the gap
    ... outside the template's envelope: its landmarks lie in the order ... the elements are not arranged as the template expects (an element not behind the one before it, a cove where none can be)
    placing the flap: a gap of ... is out of reach at an overlap of ... the asked gap and overlap cannot both hold; the message gives the smallest gap there
    block 'cove2' folds a setting past the envelope (a flap overlapping by 2 % of the chord or more under a narrow gap)

    Quality and runs¶

    On the 30P30N at 150 cells per chord (113k cells): no folded cell; the first cell within 1 % of wall_spacing and the walls orthogonal to 0.05 deg away from the trailing-edge and corner fans; cells as skewed as 13 deg where the main element's cove narrows into the flap gap; neighbouring cells across block interfaces within 1.4x of each other (median), up to 23x at the main element's cove corner. NACA-based sections (sharp and blunt trailing edges, a stepped and a smooth cove, a slat ahead of a plain main element: tests/verification/test_highlift_shapes.py) build with no cell skewed under 21 deg.

    main_flap_sa (113k cells) settles at 0, 5.5 and 9.5 deg in 3760, 3560 and 6940 iterations (3.4 to 3.8 orders), CL 2.135, 2.803, 3.041, 116 ms an iteration on a V100. The generated 30P30N at 5.5 deg: CL 2.535, 2.808, 2.846 on 75k, 113k and 165k cells, its Cp on JAXA's RANS on all three elements (validation/compare_30p30n.py); this code on JAXA's grids: 2.787 on 64k cells, 2.8756 on 261k.

    The settings round the 30P30N's (validation/highlift_envelope.py): every flap deflection from 20 to 40 deg with gaps 0.5 to 3 % and overlaps -1 to +0.14 % builds (but a 0.5 % gap, out of reach at -1 % overlap), and +2 % with a 3 % gap; a 2 % overlap under a narrower gap folds the cove block and is refused. Every slat deflection from 20 to 30 deg with gaps 1.5 to 4 % builds (under 2.4 % the overlap must come to -1 %: at its given -2.4 % the slat cannot come nearer).
