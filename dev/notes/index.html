    Design notes¶

    Why some things are done the way they are, with the measurements behind each choice, grouped by module.

    solvers/implicit.py¶

    • Defect correction is the default Jacobian. On 112x32 over 400 steps, a limited second-order residual dropped 0.06 orders with the exact Jacobian and 2.8 with the first-order one; an unlimited residual gave 4.9 orders exact. The exact Jacobian of a limited scheme carries the limiter's kinks.
    • The CFL ramp keys off the linear solve, not the residual history. A stagnated GMRES returns a null update, which leaves the residual unchanged and looks like success to a residual-based test; a freestream start makes the residual rise through the transient, which looks like failure to textbook SER (Mulder and van Leer, J. Comput. Phys. 59 (1985)). One extra matvec, about 1% of a step, measures the linear reduction instead.
    • max_relative_update. Rejection alone only stops steps that leave the physical domain. On TMR NACA 0012 at 15 deg, 449x129, accepted steps built an odd-even checkerboard in nu~ and rho within 600 steps, after which every step was rejected; bounding the relative change (0.2) cured it.

    solvers/driver.py¶

    • Force-based stopping is the default (convergence.forces_tol 1e-4, the gallery's settledness bar, applied to CL, CD and CM). On the M6 1.08M level from freestream it stops at 2540 steps with the forces within 3e-6 of the 3000-step values; 1e-5 did not stop within 3000 (the last-quarter window still holds the early drift). RANS residuals stall at round-off 10-11 orders down, and some cases at 2-3 (30P30N L1, NACA 0012 at 0 deg) with the forces settled to six digits long before. On RAE 2822 the run stops at 940 of 6000 steps with the 6000-step forces to every printed digit; on the M6 135k level at 1600, the same forces to six digits whatever the Krylov settings.

    • A frozen run stops as stalled, never settled. When every step is rejected the state does not move, so the forces are flat and would pass the settledness test (a mis-started 1.08M M6 run stopped as settled after 260 steps that way). A block whose residual is bitwise constant is no progress, whatever the equations.

    • Convergence is judged from the running peak. Started from uniform freestream, the first residual is near zero (every cell away from the wall is in equilibrium), so normalising by it reports a residual that appears to grow through the transient.

    core/config.py¶

    • float64 only. A float32 path existed and was removed. The 5.9x double-precision penalty on a 4 GB laptop card is real, but every verification gate must run in double: a suite that cannot tell a bug from round-off is no use, and no result was ever quoted from the single-precision path.
    • HLLC by default: it resolves a stationary contact exactly and needs no entropy fix, so the scheme carries no tunable dissipation.
    • Krylov sizes. The Arnoldi loop (solvers/krylov.py) runs to full length whether or not it has converged, testing only between restarts, so a large subspace is paid in full every step: small subspace (10), two restarts. Inexact Newton tolerance 1e-2 (Eisenstat and Walker, SIAM J. Sci. Comput. 17 (1996)).
    • CFL ramp defaults. Start at 10 (five times what the explicit path tolerates); growth capped at 1.3 per step and earned by the residual reduction; cfl_max is a backstop, not a tuning knob: the ramp settles at whatever the preconditioner sustains, limited in practice by krylov_success.

    numerics/riemann.py¶

    • Why HLLC. Roe's scheme is shock-unstable on high-aspect-ratio cells, and the TMR grids reach such ratios at the shock foot: on 448x128 at M 0.8 the transonic shock never settled (CL oscillating 0.04-0.28), curable only by an entropy-fix coefficient large enough to bias CL by 16%. HLLC takes its dissipation from wave-speed estimates, has no vanishing eigenvalue, and needs no fix. On the O-grid at M 0.8, alpha 1.25, 128 squared, the two agree to three digits (CL 0.33567 vs 0.33585, CD 0.025711 vs 0.025698, observed orders 0.94/1.37 vs 0.92/1.33), so Roe stays as the independent check, 15% cheaper per iteration.
    • Contact resolution is required: a slip wall has vn = 0 along its length, so a flux that smears contacts smears exactly where CL and CD are computed. A Rusanov entry was built and removed for that reason.

    numerics/reconstruction.py, numerics/limiters.py¶

    • Raw index differences, not spacing-weighted gradients. Limiting a gradient over the real cell spacings is the formally correct second-order form on a graded grid; it was built, measured and removed. On the strongly graded TMR C-grids it lost badly (transonic CL 0.1854 against 0.2461, oscillation 4.5x larger): the TVD region of a limiter depends on the spacing ratio and these limiters ignore it. On the mildly graded conformal O-grids it lost marginally, for no gain in order (CD 0.003103 against 0.002831 at 128 squared, observed order 1.16 against 1.13). The weighted variant is in the git history.
    • Limiters take differences (a, b), not ratios: no 0/0 on a flat field, no guard epsilon. Every limiter must return exactly a at a == b (second order in smooth flow); this is unit-tested.
    • minmod vs van Albada. On the explicit path on TMR's C-grids minmod held a transonic shock where van Albada reached 2.29 orders against minmod's 4.39 (later confounded by those grids' own limit cycle). Van Albada came back for RAE 2822: smooth where active, close to the central slope in smooth flow. Van Leer is in the git history.
    • Venkatakrishnan is deliberately absent: it is a smooth Barth-Jespersen for unstructured grids, not a slope-ratio limiter; fed r = a/b it returns phi(1) = 3/4, quietly losing second order.

    mesh/coarsen.py¶

    • Grid sequencing coarsens the case's own grid (every other node, the segments' indices halved), so it needs no second grid file and works for generated, file and multi-block grids alike. Injection (each child takes its parent's value) is enough: from the settled 135k M6 level, the 1.08M level had its forces within 1e-4 of the 3000-step values for good from step 620, against 1760 from freestream. The canvas padding cells must keep the freestream start (a zero density there fails every step's physicality test).

    core/linalg.py¶

    • The line solve is block cyclic reduction, Thomas kept as the reference in the tests. A Thomas sweep is 2L dependent steps per application (L the chain length), each a small kernel: on an A100 the preconditioner was 89% of an RAE 2822 Newton step (121.7 against 13.6 ms with full-length GMRES). Odd-even reduction is ceil(log2 L) levels for about twice the flops: per apply 3.52 -> 0.136 ms on RAE 2822 (flow chains of 128), 2.08 -> 0.24 ms on M6 135k, and still faster at 1.08M cells (4.81 -> 2.89 ms) once the power-of-two padding was dropped (with it, 1.08M was no faster). lax.scan(unroll=8) gave only 2-4x and was dropped.

    solvers/krylov.py¶

    • Right preconditioning. On TMR's 112x32 C-grid at CFL 1000, ten left-preconditioned GMRES iterations returned a true residual 3.1x larger than they started with; ten right-preconditioned ones 0.47x (0.18x with the lines continued through the wake cut).

    mesh/atlas.py¶

    • Line chains stop at other blocks. Continued through every interface, 30P30N L1 ran 2.1x slower per step (chains of 190 cells instead of 66) for no convergence gain; only a block's own j-cut (the C-grid wake) is crossed.

    mesh/highlift.py, mesh/blocking.py (multi-element grids)¶

    Measured on the 30P30N's elements at 150 wall cells per chord (75.9k cells main + flap, 113k all three), quality as tests/unit/test_highlift.py measures it.

    • One chain template, not three. An O-layer's outer contour is rounded at every trailing edge (and the 30P30N's are blunt), so a cut leaving the nose tip meets the contour tangentially: blocks cannot be split there. The families are compositions per element pair: a slot block over the downstream element (left side the gap cut and the upstream base, top the upstream wake), a cove block (upstream lip to base, gap cut, downstream nose, mouth), a drop from every lip. Fill blocks have j from a wall where they touch one.
    • The gap is a side, never a chain member. With the gap cut in the chain of one block over everything, 80 i-lines packed into the 0.005 gap ran up to the middle loop and Winslow's psi lost authority there (cells of 0.004-0.007 against 3e-4 wanted, 95x across the cut). As a side of both the cove and the slot it is 5-6x at worst, median 3.
    • Wakes are offsets of the slot's floor. A Hermite wake from a slat's trailing edge cannot follow the main element's surface; the floor (the downstream contour and wake) offset node for node, by the gap growing 10 % per unit length, does, and gives the slot straight near-normal lines. The normals are smoothed over two offsets and the curve over one, then the nodes put back at their arc fractions (averaging alone slid them: 12.7x at the main element's base); unsmoothed, the flap's base creased the main wake (17-24x across it).
    • Connectors fixed, each block smoothed alone. Smoothing all blocks at once with the connectors' nodes free (stencils across blocks) let corners collapse where three blocks meet (the slat cusp folded); anchoring the cuts' ends folded where anchored met free. Fixed Hermite cuts with spacing matched at their ends: no fold in any family.
    • Spacing control, iterated. Thomas-Middlecoff psi interpolated from a block's sides ignores the layer it sits on (first cells 10-20x the layer's last step mid-block). psi from the heights wanted off the bottom and top (per j line, from its end nodes alone) fixed the medians; three GRAPE-style rounds (heights rescaled by sqrt(wanted/got)) brought the concave-corner peak from 26.6x to 22.8x and medians to 1.1-1.4; more rounds skewed the cove and slot (12 rounds: 11 deg) and cost 100 s.
    • Near-wall layer. Hyperbolic marching folds into a concave corner (the main element's cove step) at any thickness above about a wall cell: its smoothing is raised 31x there, ramped from zero at the wall so the first steps stay normal; a residual fold halves the thickness round it and marches again. A sharp convex corner (the slat cusp, 18 deg) twisted its first cells until the corner's two neighbours were placed equally far (the step is normal to the chord through them). The march's smoothing at the wall tilted the first step 5-8 deg on the slat and main noses; from zero at the wall (except within two nodes of a convex corner, whose fan needs it) the wall is orthogonal to 0.05 deg. The first cell is re-marched until within 1 % of the wall spacing (up to 6 passes; the marching's area smoothing moved it 3-12 %).
    • Sharp trailing edges. A sharp trailing edge is the layer's seam node: the convex-corner test (heading jumps, unwrapped) missed it, the wall got no smoothing there and the layer folded, then was thinned to the floor and refused (every sharp main element with a flap). Turning is taken from each node's two edges instead, and the edge's two neighbours placed equally far, as at the slat cusp. NACA-based sections then build at 22-38 deg.
    • Targets not met yet. Smallest angle 20 deg: 17.4 deg at the main element's concave cove corner (one layer cell), 12.9 deg where the cove narrows into the flap gap (66 cells), 16.4 deg where the slot's left side turns from the gap cut onto the upstream base. Interface growth 1.3: 22.8x at the concave corner (the layer is thinned there and the cove's lines fan), 5-7x round the gap; medians 1.1-1.4. Tried and dropped: splitting the cove at its corner (to the downstream nose in proportion, or at its nearest point: slivers of 0.1-7.5 deg), and starting the slot where the channel narrows to three gaps (its left side then ran along the ceiling, parallel to its floor, and folded).

    3D¶

    • Face vectors from the diagonals' cross product, S = (x_c - x_a) x (x_d - x_b) / 2: the vector area of every surface the face's edges bound, so a cell's six vectors sum to zero exactly on warped faces and a collapsed face (a pole) is exactly zero. Volumes (1/3) sum S . x_f equal the trilinear hexahedron's (tested against Gauss quadrature).
    • Face gradients: the 2D diamond generalized (centre-to-centre vector plus the face's two diagonals, node values and positions both averaged from the 8 surrounding centres), not Green-Gauss on a dual cell: exact for linear fields on any hexahedra, the same property and code as 2D.
    • The pole is an interface, i_min facing itself half a turn round the O seam: the ghost across the axis is the diametrically opposite cell. Its faces have zero area, so it only feeds slopes and gradients.
    • Plane-reflection ghosts on skewed cells (walls, symmetry; 2D and 3D): the face state is off by O(h tan theta) on a cell skewed by theta, an O(1) local truncation error. Measured with the 3D MMS on a warped cube: first order in the solution with Dirichlet ghosts placed that way, second order with ghosts at the cells of linearly extrapolated nodes (a point reflection through the face midpoint). Kept for the physical conditions (standard; the M6 wall is within 2 deg of orthogonal except at the sharp trailing edge and the leading-edge seam); the MMS uses the latter.
    • 3D incidence is in x-z, span y (TMR's wing grids): freestream_primitive(flow, 3) is M (cos a, 0, sin a); a 2D grid extruded in z needs the freestream passed explicitly (tests/verification/test_extruded.py does).
